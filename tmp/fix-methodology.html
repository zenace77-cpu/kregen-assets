K-Regen Lab assesses what product disclosures establish, converts usable figures into comparable units and states what remains unknown.

What this methodology measures

This methodology evaluates the information published about a Korean PDRN skincare product. It does not measure whether the product works. What is assessed is disclosure transparency, not efficacy.

A product can disclose its ingredients clearly without proving that it performs well. It can also have limited disclosure without being a poor formula. The verdict reflects what can be checked from the available information.

How a product is assessed

  1. Collect the basic product information. We record the full ingredient list, or INCI, shown by the retailer, along with the bottle size, current price and date checked.
  2. Identify the concentration claim. We find any concentration figure published by the brand and determine what it describes. It may refer to an isolated ingredient, a proprietary or branded complex, an exosome count, or nothing identifiable at the front of the label.
  3. Check the claim against the ingredient list. We look for a named DNA-derived ingredient in the INCI and record where it appears. Its position provides context but does not establish its concentration.
  4. Convert usable information into comparable units. We calculate price per milliliter. We calculate cost per milligram of disclosed active only when a published concentration can be attributed to the active itself.
  5. Publish the figures and the gaps. Every figure is accompanied by the date checked. Missing, ambiguous or conflicting information is identified rather than estimated.

Ingredient terms are not interchangeable

Product pages use several terms that can appear similar while describing different materials or claims. K-Regen Lab keeps these terms separate.

Sodium DNA

Sodium DNA is an INCI ingredient name that may identify a DNA-derived material in the formula. Its presence confirms that the ingredient is listed. It does not establish the material's source, molecular characteristics or concentration unless those details are separately disclosed.

Hydrolyzed DNA

Hydrolyzed DNA is a different ingredient name. It indicates DNA that has been broken into smaller fragments. It is not automatically treated as identical to Sodium DNA or to every material marketed as PDRN.

PDRN

PDRN is commonly used in product marketing, but the term on the front of a product does not by itself identify the exact INCI ingredient or its concentration. The supporting ingredient disclosure must be checked.

Plant-derived branded terminology

Some brands use plant-derived branded "PDRN" terminology. That terminology is not treated as interchangeable with a disclosed DNA-derived ingredient. The analysis reports what the brand calls the material and what the ingredient list actually names.

PDRN-containing complexes

A branded complex may contain PDRN alongside water, botanical extracts, solvents or other ingredients. A percentage or ppm figure for the entire complex is not the concentration of PDRN alone. K-Regen Lab does not convert a complex claim into a single-ingredient concentration.

Raw material and finished product

A raw material can have its own concentration before it is added to a formula. That figure is not automatically the concentration in the finished product. A finished-product concentration must be disclosed separately or supported by information that clearly applies to the product in the bottle.

What the analysis distinguishes

Products are not badged with a grade. The analysis separates the following situations and says which one applies in the text of the article itself, because the distinction between them is usually the whole point of the article.

  • A concentration is disclosed and the ingredient list presents no obvious conflict. The figure can be checked and used in a cost-per-milligram calculation.
  • The ingredient is listed but no concentration is published. This is the most common case in the category, and articles say so plainly rather than filling the gap with an estimate.
  • The advertised figure describes a branded or multi-ingredient complex rather than the active alone. The figure is reported as what it measures, and it is not converted into a single-ingredient concentration.
  • Public disclosures conflict. Different sources give materially different figures that cannot be reconciled from what is published. The conflict is reported as a conflict, not resolved in the brand's favour.
  • A headline claim exists but cannot be tied to a specific ingredient. The claim is reported alongside what the ingredient list does and does not support.

None of these describe clinical benefit, formulation quality or suitability for an individual reader. They describe how far the available disclosure allows a claim to be checked.

Limits of the method

No products are tested. K-Regen Lab operates no laboratory, conducts no trial and publishes no before-and-after testing. The articles are not first-hand accounts of product use.

Ingredient presence, formulation, clinical evidence and actual product performance are four different things. They are never treated as one. Finding an ingredient in an INCI list does not demonstrate how the complete formula performs on skin.

A higher concentration does not automatically mean better results. Performance can depend on the complete formulation and factors that a public ingredient list cannot establish.

Retailer copy is not independent scientific evidence. It is a source for what a seller publicly discloses about a product. It does not independently prove efficacy.

Prices change. Promotions, availability and retailer pricing can move quickly. Every price carries the date it was checked and may already be stale when an article is read.

Ingredient-list position shows order, not amount. Position can provide context, but it cannot support a precise ppm, percentage or cost-per-milligram calculation.

These boundaries are part of the method. They prevent a limited product disclosure from being presented as stronger evidence than it is. Related publication rules appear in the editorial policy. Health-related limits appear in the medical disclaimer.
