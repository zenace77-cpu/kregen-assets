What's actually in the Medicube PDRN Pink Collagen serums

Medicube sells the Pink Collagen Exosome Shot Serum in two versions, and the gap between them is narrower than the names suggest.

  • Pink Collagen Exosome Shot Serum 2000 — 30ml, $21.90 ($0.73/ml). The label states "2000ppm of exosomes, salmon PDRN and collagen." The PDRN-relevant INCI is Sodium DNA, sitting in the middle of the ingredient list.
  • Pink Collagen Exosome Shot Serum 7500 — 30ml, $23.90 (about $0.80/ml). The label states "7500ppm of exosomes." Same PDRN-relevant INCI: Sodium DNA, again mid-list.

The finding that matters most, and that most reviews skip: neither product discloses a PDRN concentration. The 2,000 and 7,500 figures describe exosome content. On the 2000 version the number is attached to a bundle — "exosomes, salmon PDRN and collagen" — so it's a blended-complex figure, not a PDRN figure. On the 7500 version the brand is even clearer that the number refers to exosomes.

So the serum carries a PDRN name and a big number in the product name, and that number is not a PDRN number. This isn't a Medicube-specific problem — it runs through the whole category, as the comparisons below show — but buyers reasonably assume "7500" means more PDRN, and there's no published figure to support that.

What ingredient position does and doesn't tell you

Cosmetic ingredient lists are ordered by descending concentration for everything present above 1%. That single rule is the most useful tool a shopper has. Sodium DNA sitting mid-list in both Pink Collagen serums suggests it isn't a trace afterthought buried behind fragrance. It does not, however, establish a figure or an upper bound: ingredients below roughly 1% may be listed in any order, and the 1% line is invisible. Without a disclosed number, "mid-list" is all you get, and saying so plainly beats guessing.

Pink Collagen 2000 vs 7500: is the upgrade worth $2?

Strictly on published data: the 7500 version costs about 9% more for 3.75x the advertised exosome figure. If exosome content is what you're after, that reads as reasonable value. If PDRN is what you're after, the two products are indistinguishable on paper, because neither publishes a PDRN concentration. Anyone paying the premium specifically for "more salmon DNA" is paying for a number that doesn't measure salmon DNA.

The Medicube product that actually discloses its PDRN: Pink Peptide Serum

How the Medicube PDRN formulas compare to their rivals

We priced and read the ingredient lists for 20 PDRN-branded serums and ampoules on 2026-08-08. Two patterns dominate.

1. Price per ml barely moves; cost per mg of active swings wildly

Most of these products cluster between $0.25 and $1.00 per ml. That narrow band hides a huge spread in what you're actually paying for:

  • Genabelle PDRN 3% Hyper Boost Ampoule — 40ml, $29.70 ($0.74/ml). Label says 3% PDRN; INCI is Sodium DNA; disclosed PDRN 30,000ppm. That's roughly $0.025 per mg — the best ratio in the set.
  • Medicube PDRN Pink Peptide Serum — 1% disclosed, about $0.08 per mg.
  • AVCA PDRN Lifting Ampoule — 50ml, $12.72 ($0.25/ml), label says 1,000ppm. Cheapest per ml in its tier, but about $0.25 per mg. Worth noting: the 2-pack listing for the same product states 20,000ppm. The brand contradicts itself across its own listings.
  • Anua PDRN Hyaluronic Acid Capsule 100 Serum — 30ml, $21 ($0.70/ml), disclosed PDRN 100ppm, which works out to roughly $7 per mg.

Same shelf, similar price per ml, and the cost per mg of disclosed PDRN differs by orders of magnitude. Setting aside one extreme outlier (below), the spread across advertised figures is roughly 280x. Price per ml tells you almost nothing in this category.

2. The biggest number on the label is usually the least informative

Sort the data by headline ppm and a pattern shows up fast: the loudest claims are the ones that don't survive a read of the ingredient list.

  • PESTLO T3 PDRN Exosome Glow Ampoule — 30ml, $30.75. Advertises "high concentration salmon PDRN complex 150,000ppm," the largest number in the data set. But that describes a complex, not PDRN content, and Sodium DNA still appears near the end of the INCI.
  • VT PDRN Essence 100 — 30ml, $28. Claims "100,000ppm low-molecular ginseng-derived PDRN," or 10%. Sodium DNA is the last ingredient listed, which caps it well below that, while Panax Ginseng Root Extract sits near the top. The headline number most plausibly describes the ginseng extract.
  • SWANICOCO PDRN Hydrating Repair Ampoule — 15ml (5ml x 3), $49.38 ($3.29/ml), by far the highest price per ml here. Claims 20,000ppm salmon PDRN, but Sodium DNA is listed after fragrance, which is hard to square with a 2% claim.
  • APLB PDRN Vitamin C Ampoule Serum — 40ml, $9.80. "49.1% PDRN VITA CEN" sounds enormous; it describes a blended complex of salmon PDRN, vitamin C and centella, not PDRN content.
  • be the skin Vitavita Raspberry PDRN Ampoule — 35ml, $16.90. This is the starkest one in the set: the page markets "20,000ppm small molecular PDRN" while its own ingredient list prints "Sodium DNA(2ppm)" — a 10,000x gap between marketing and declaration, on the same page. At 2ppm, the cost lands near $241 per mg, and that single product is the source of the most extreme ratio in our data.

Against that backdrop, Medicube's Pink Collagen serums look mid-pack rather than bad. They don't overclaim a PDRN percentage the ingredient list can't support. They just lead with a number that measures a different ingredient.

Watch for "PDRN" that isn't PDRN

Several products marketed as PDRN don't use salmon-derived Sodium DNA at all. innisfree Retinol Green Tea PDRN Skin Booster Ampoule (25ml, $30.50) advertises "low-molecular Green Tea PDRN," but the INCI is Camellia Sinensis Leaf Extract — a plant extract, not polydeoxyribonucleotide. Isntree Gim PDRN Hydro Bouncy Ampoule leans on seaweed-derived branding, and VT's Essence 100 on ginseng. Medicube's serums, whatever you make of the numbering, do list Sodium DNA.

The quiet products are sometimes the better ones

Two products in the set publish no ppm figure at all, yet place Sodium DNA 7th in the INCI — earlier than several products shouting five- and six-figure numbers. COSNORI PDRN Revitalizing Ampoule (50ml, $23, $0.46/ml) and Isntree Gim PDRN Hydro Bouncy Ampoule (50ml, $16.24, $0.33/ml) both land in that camp. Silence on the label isn't evidence of a weak formula.

For context: REJURAN Turnover Ampoule (30ml, $52) — from the brand behind the well-known injectable — is itself an over-the-counter topical cosmetic, not a version of the injectable treatment. It uses Hydrolyzed DNA rather than Sodium DNA and discloses no concentration.

Who the Medicube PDRN Pink Collagen formula suits — and who should skip it

It makes sense if

  • You want a lightweight daily serum in the $22–$24 range and you're interested in the exosome + collagen + PDRN blend as a whole, rather than chasing one number.
  • You value a brand with wide US retail availability and a clear returns path over a small-batch import.
  • You're layering it under a moisturizer as a hydration and texture step, with cosmetic expectations rather than treatment expectations.

Skip it if

  • You're buying PDRN by the milligram. Neither Pink Collagen version discloses a PDRN figure, so you can't calculate value. The Genabelle 3% ampoule (about $0.025/mg) or Medicube's own Pink Peptide Serum (about $0.08/mg) are the transparent choices.
  • You assumed "7500" meant more salmon DNA than "2000." It doesn't — it's an exosome figure.
  • You're fragrance-sensitive or easily reactive. Check the full ingredient list on the retailer page before buying, since formulas change between batches, and patch test first.
