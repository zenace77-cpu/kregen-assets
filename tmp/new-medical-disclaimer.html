This article is general information about cosmetic ingredients. It is not medical advice and it is not a substitute for diagnosis or treatment by a qualified clinician.

What this site covers

K-Regen Lab covers over-the-counter topical cosmetics. These include serums, ampoules and creams applied at home. No product mentioned on this site is intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease or condition.

The site analyzes published ingredient lists, concentration disclosures, product sizes and prices. It does not test products or report first-hand use. Nothing published here characterizes how a product will feel, absorb or behave on any individual's skin.

Topical products are not injectable procedures

Injectable PDRN and polynucleotide procedures performed by licensed clinicians belong to a different regulatory category. They also use a different delivery method and reach a different depth of action.

Nothing on K-Regen Lab should be read as guidance on injectable PDRN, injectable polynucleotides or any other in-office procedure. Comparisons between topical cosmetics and injectable procedures do not establish equivalent effects.

Individual reactions

Skin reactions vary between individuals. Patch testing is the reader's own decision to make. A product that is tolerated by one person may not be tolerated by another.

Reported discomfort with products in this category frequently traces to other components of the formula rather than to the DNA-derived ingredient itself. Fragrance, solvents, exfoliating ingredients and other formula components may affect tolerability. This is a reason to read the complete ingredient list rather than focusing on one highlighted ingredient.

Stop using a product if you experience a severe or persistent reaction and seek medical attention.

When to consult a clinician

Consult a board-certified dermatologist or another qualified clinician before adding or changing anything in your routine if you:

  • Have a diagnosed or suspected skin condition
  • Have active acne or rosacea
  • Have a compromised skin barrier
  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Are considering or comparing an in-office procedure

A clinician can assess circumstances that cannot be evaluated from a public ingredient list or a general article.

How to read the site

Product disclosures can establish what a brand or retailer publishes. They cannot predict an individual medical outcome. Concentration, ingredient presence, formulation quality and clinical performance are separate questions.

The review methodology explains how product disclosures are compared. The editorial policy explains the rules applied to claims, sources and corrections.
